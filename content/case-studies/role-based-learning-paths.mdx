## Context

The Digital Learning Zone assigns learning according to role and function. When an associate changes position, the path attached to an earlier role may no longer reflect the work they are expected to perform.

This is an administrative task with a judgement problem inside it. A role title alone may not contain enough information for a reliable assignment.

## Challenge

The work required consistency without pretending that every case was clear. The main risks were mapping from incomplete role data, applying different logic to similar roles, and treating a clean spreadsheet as proof that the underlying information was correct.

The strongest decision in this work was to escalate an unclear case instead of quietly completing the row.

## What I designed

I used a role-mapping structure to compare available associate information with function-based learning paths. Cases that could not be matched with confidence were flagged for clarification.

A safe public artifact should show the fields that supported the decision, such as a redacted identifier, department, current role, proposed path, decision status, decision reason and authorised owner. The final field list must match the working file before publication.

## How I ran it

The reviewed process was to gather changed-role information, compare it with available learning paths, record a proposed match where the source supported one, and escalate cases where it did not.

The final count, approver role, system update step and one anonymised exception example still need confirmation.

## Outcome and evidence

The available evidence supports a documented review and escalation principle. It does not yet support a claim that capability, completion or business performance improved.

This case also separates a completed mapping activity from a confirmed system update. Those are different states and need different evidence.

> A completed row is not a reliable decision when the role data beneath it remains unclear.

## What I would do differently

### Agree the decision rules first

I would define the authoritative role source, the differences that require a new path, the cases L&D can decide and the cases that need department confirmation.

### Record exceptions

I would add reason codes, effective dates and a named decision owner. Repeated ambiguity could then reveal whether the gap sits in the learning catalogue, the role data or the handover process.

### Audit after the update

Thirty days later, I would check whether the intended path was assigned, obsolete learning remained attached and the associate could access the required learning.
