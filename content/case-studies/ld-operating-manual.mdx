## Context

Learning and Development work at a live hotel property includes recurring programmes, compliance activity, templates, reporting and handover. The information required for that work had been distributed across six knowledge sources.

My task was to organise the available material into one reference for day-to-day work. The scope was property-level operating knowledge. It did not replace formal company standards or set a complete L&D strategy.

## Challenge

The visible problem was fragmentation. The deeper problem was deciding what belonged together, what needed to remain separate and how another person would find the correct instruction at the moment of use.

A long document can solve a storage problem while creating a navigation problem. The manual therefore needed a structure that connected standards, templates and recurring actions.

## What I designed

I produced a 150-page operating manual that consolidated material from six candidate-reported sources. It covered standards, templates and recurring work.

The public evidence package still needs four redacted artifacts:

1. An information map showing how the original sources were grouped.
2. A contents page showing the hierarchy and naming system.
3. One workflow spread showing how an operator moves from context to action.
4. A template reference showing how a recurring task connects to its working file.

## How I ran it

The final workflow is still under evidence review. Before this page enters public navigation, I will confirm how the sources were collected, how conflicts were handled, who reviewed the content and whether another person tested a task using the manual.

## Outcome and evidence

The available evidence currently supports three output claims: one operating manual, 150 candidate-reported pages and six candidate-reported sources.

Adoption and business effect were not measured in a form that can be reported here. This page does not claim time saved, fewer errors, faster onboarding or improved audit results.

> Page count proves production effort. It does not prove that another person can find and use the right instruction.

## What I would do differently

### Separate the maintenance layers

I would divide a next version into a concise operating guide, a linked template library and a change log with a named owner and review date. Each layer has a different update cycle.

### Test retrieval

I would ask a colleague who did not build the manual to complete three recurring tasks using only the guide. Their stops, searches and label choices would guide the next revision.

### Define adoption evidence at launch

I would agree a small measurement set before release: tasks supported, users who accessed the guide, questions raised after use and findability after a real task.
