## Context

For Marriott Culture Week 2026, I created a working team challenge intended for a shared room. The strongest supplied artifact is Obstacle Challenge, a browser-based crossword race designed for projection and MC control.

The exact event dates, live use, audience size and boundaries between my contribution and the wider team still need confirmation before this page enters public navigation.

## Challenge

A culture event can become passive when the audience only listens. A game can also become difficult to run when it has too many rules, unclear scoring or no reliable operator view.

Obstacle Challenge needed to support two users at once: teams that needed clear rules and visible progress, and an MC who needed to manage questions, time, answers and scores from one interface.

## What I designed

The supplied HTML verifies these mechanics:

1. Four colour-coded teams.
2. Ten clue rows and one hidden ten-letter keyword.
3. A 20-second timer for each question.
4. Ten points for a correctly spelled answer.
5. One Hope Star decision per team, worth 20 points when correct and minus 10 when wrong.
6. A 50-point hidden-keyword attempt.
7. Operator controls for the question, timer, answer and round state.
8. Score, results and final recognition screens.

These details describe the product as built. They do not describe participant response or learning effect.

## How I designed the facilitation flow

The interaction moved from a team introduction and rule briefing to clue selection, timed shared answers, score recording and a final result. The operator view kept the activity sequence in one place.

## Outcome and evidence

The working artifact proves that one interactive game with ten clues, four designed teams and a 20-second question timer was built.

Live use and participant response have not been confirmed. No survey, knowledge check or behaviour measure has been supplied. Game score is not treated as evidence of culture understanding.

> The artifact proves the interaction was built. It does not prove that the intended learning effect occurred.

## What I would do differently

### Reduce the rule load

I would test the rules with a small group and remove any mechanic that does not support the learning objective. A one-screen summary would remain visible beside the board.

### Rehearse the operator flow

I would create a short rehearsal, a one-page MC control guide, a printed scoring backup and a static fallback for a browser or projector issue.

### Measure the learning objective

I would add three short scenario questions or a team reflection after the activity. The case could then report recall or application separately from points and participation.

### Improve accessibility

I would pair colour with team names and patterns, label every control, confirm keyboard operation, reduce motion and compress the asset package.
