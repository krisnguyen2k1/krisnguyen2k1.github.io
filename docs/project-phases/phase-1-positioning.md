# Phase 1 - Positioning

## Positioning objective

The site should position Kris for L&D Specialist roles in large multinational or enterprise organisations. It should not imply people management, budget ownership, enterprise LMS implementation leadership or a senior title that Kris has not held.

The competitive move is to replace a years-of-experience comparison with an evidence-of-systems comparison.

## Territory 1 - The learning systems builder

**One-line positioning statement**

> I turn operating needs into learning systems people can use and leaders can trust.

**What this territory means**

Kris is not presented primarily as a trainer. He is presented as an early-career L&D practitioner who notices where learning operations depend on memory, scattered files, unclear ownership or unexamined assumptions, then turns them into repeatable infrastructure.

**Proof required to hold it**

- A redacted sample from the 150-page L&D operating manual showing how scattered sources became one usable operating reference.
- A role-mapping artifact showing the logic used to map learning requirements and how ambiguous cases were escalated.
- A before-and-after view of one recurring process, such as orientation delivery, trainee tracking or statutory training administration.
- Evidence that another person could use the system without Kris present.
- At least one adoption or operating-effect signal: time saved, fewer errors, faster handover, completion improvement or manager usage. `[NEEDS INPUT]`

**Audience it wins**

- Heads of L&D and Talent Development hiring an L&D Specialist who can own a workstream.
- Operations-heavy multinationals in hospitality, retail, manufacturing, logistics, banking and technology-enabled services.
- Hiring managers who need documentation, governance, data discipline and execution rather than facilitation alone.
- Recruiters who can accept early career when the work is inspectable and unusually structured.

**Audience it loses**

- Employers seeking five or more years of people management, budget ownership or enterprise learning strategy.
- Roles centred almost entirely on motivational facilitation or event delivery.
- Hiring managers who interpret `systems` as software implementation unless the portfolio clearly defines it as operating infrastructure.

**Strategic risk**

Without artifacts and evidence of adoption, `systems` becomes another abstract claim. This territory only works if the case studies make the systems visible.

## Territory 2 - The digital learning product builder

**One-line positioning statement**

> I design and ship digital learning products that make complex knowledge easier to navigate, practise and apply.

**What this territory means**

Kris is presented as someone who can independently move from research and content architecture to interaction design and a working web product. The eight published websites and Obstacle Challenge become the centre of the brand.

**Proof required to hold it**

- The working Obstacle Challenge with a concise explanation of audience, learning objective, interaction model and facilitation context.
- Kris's English Studio as evidence of practice design and repeatable learner interaction.
- Ho Chi Minh City Labour Market 2026 as evidence of data storytelling.
- Krishnamurti: A Human Life as evidence of long-form research, source architecture and information design.
- A clear account of what Kris personally researched, wrote, designed and built.
- Evidence of learner or reader usage. `[NEEDS INPUT]`

**Audience it wins**

- Digital learning, learning experience design and instructional design teams.
- Technology, fintech and innovation-oriented companies that value people who can prototype and ship independently.
- Hiring managers interested in microlearning, interactive content and digital enablement.

**Audience it loses**

- Traditional HR teams that mainly need classroom delivery, compliance administration or stakeholder coordination.
- Senior learning technology roles expecting SCORM, xAPI, authoring-suite depth, product analytics or enterprise LMS implementation experience.
- Recruiters who may mistake the projects for personal publishing unless every project is connected to a workplace learning capability.

**Strategic risk**

This territory could move Kris out of the L&D Specialist category and into a junior web or content-creator category. It is powerful as differentiation but too narrow as the primary position.

## Territory 3 - The behaviour and operations translator

**One-line positioning statement**

> I connect hospitality operations, psychology and learning design to help people perform more consistently at work.

**What this territory means**

Kris is presented as someone who understands both the person learning and the operation requiring performance. Hospitality, education, psychology and L&D become a coherent professional story.

**Proof required to hold it**

- A case where an operating problem was translated into a behavioural objective rather than a content request.
- Orientation or Food Safety materials showing bilingual and shift-aware design.
- Facilitation photographs with accurate context, audience and Kris's role.
- A verified account of psychology education and how it changed a real design decision.
- Behaviour, supervisor-observation or consistency data. `[NEEDS INPUT]`

**Audience it wins**

- Hospitality groups, service organisations and employee-experience teams.
- Hiring managers responsible for onboarding, service culture, early-career talent or frontline capability.
- Leaders who value facilitation and human judgement alongside operational discipline.

**Audience it loses**

- Data-heavy or technology-heavy L&D teams looking for measurement and systems ownership.
- Enterprise recruiters who may read `psychology` and `human` as soft unless business evidence immediately follows.
- General corporate roles where the hospitality framing appears too sector-specific.

**Strategic risk**

This is the easiest territory to make warm and credible, but also the easiest to make generic. It does not solve the experience-gap problem as strongly as Territory 1.

## Recommendation

### Choose Territory 1 - The learning systems builder

Use this as the primary market position:

> I turn operating needs into learning systems people can use and leaders can trust.

This territory is the best fit for five reasons.

1. **It matches the target role.** An enterprise L&D Specialist is expected to own more than delivery. The role often requires needs analysis, design, documentation, coordination, governance and measurement.
2. **It is supported by the strongest workplace evidence.** The manual, role mapping, orientation script, trainee tracker, delegation matrix, compliance work and trainer network all belong to one coherent story.
3. **It reframes early career without hiding it.** The argument is not that Kris has senior tenure. The argument is that he has already produced systems that are unusually inspectable for his career stage.
4. **It gives the digital projects the correct role.** They become proof that Kris can independently research, structure and ship, rather than changing the professional category from L&D to web creator.
5. **It can grow with the career.** The same territory can later absorb measurement, budgets, technology decisions and team leadership without requiring a complete personal-brand reset.

Territory 2 should become the main differentiator underneath the position. Territory 3 should guide the tone and design philosophy. They should not be presented as three equal pillars.

## Evidence hierarchy

The site should use proof in this order:

1. **Artifact** - what the recruiter can inspect.
2. **Decision** - why Kris designed it that way.
3. **Adoption** - who used it and in what operating context.
4. **Effect** - what became faster, clearer, more consistent or more measurable.
5. **Reflection** - what Kris would change next time.

An output number without these layers should not be presented as an outcome.

## Positioning guardrails

### Lead with

- Operating needs.
- Repeatable learning systems.
- Inspectable artifacts.
- Decisions and trade-offs.
- Independent digital production as supporting evidence.
- Honest career stage.

### Do not lead with

- `Building toward L&D Manager`.
- A career roadmap.
- AI as an identity.
- Generic `human-centred / AI-enabled / business-minded` pillars.
- Reading volume or GPA.
- Future capability stated as present experience.
